# Creative IP Fundraising Structures

Research compiled for dezibel fundraising planning. Covers single-title funding, IP/world funding, graduation paths, SAFE mechanics at $8M cap, and comparable valuations.

---

## 1. Single Title Funding — How Individual Creative Works Get Funded

### Indie Film: The SPV Model (Industry Standard)

The dominant structure for funding a single creative work is the **Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV)** — a standalone LLC created solely for that project.

- **What investors own:** Membership units in the SPV LLC. The SPV owns the film (or IP). Investors are non-managing members (passive). The creator/producer is the managing member (creative control).
- **Typical structure:** Manager-managed LLC. Managing member runs production. Non-managing members are investors with no creative control. This is a legally clean line.
- **Revenue waterfall:** Typically: (1) return of capital to investors, (2) preferred return (often 10-20%), (3) profit split (commonly 50/50 between investors and producers after recoupment).
- **Risk isolation:** The SPV's liabilities stay inside the SPV. If the project fails, only SPV assets are at risk — not the creator's other work or future projects.
- **IP ownership sits in the SPV.** This is critical. If the project succeeds and the IP can travel (sequels, merchandise, adaptations), the investor still has a piece.

**Source:** Julian Haffner (entertainment lawyer), "Structuring a Film Raise: SPV, SAFE, or Shares?" — the definitive comparison for indie raises.

### Indie Games: Self-Fund Then Raise

Most indie game studios self-fund prototypes, then raise for production. Early-stage game investment is hits-driven — a single title can return 1000%+ or zero. Typical seed for a 3-person indie studio: ~$600K cash buffer. Revenue multiples are unreliable because game revenue fluctuates with launch cycles.

### Debut Novels with Product Ecosystems

No established structure exists because this category barely exists. Novels are traditionally advance-funded by publishers ($5K-$500K+ depending on author profile). The publisher takes distribution rights; the author retains copyright but grants exclusive licenses. No investor buys equity in a single novel.

**Dezibel doesn't fit the novel model.** It's closer to an indie film + subscription product + merchandise ecosystem. The SPV or SAFE-into-entity models are more appropriate than any publishing precedent.

## 2. IP/World Funding — Multiple Revenue Streams Under One Roof

### A24 — The Label Model (Founded 2012)

- **Seed funding:** Provided by Guggenheim Partners (Daniel Katz, co-founder, led Guggenheim's film finance group). Some sources cite Eldridge Industries as early minority stakeholder.
- **Early model:** Not a single-IP company. A24 launched as a **distribution and production label** from day one. Lower-cost digital marketing and social media instead of traditional advertising.
- **First major equity raise:** 2022 — $225M at $2.5B valuation, led by Stripes with Neuberger Berman. Founders and employees retained majority equity.
- **2024:** Thrive Capital invested $75M. 2024 valuation: $3.5B.
- **Key insight:** A24 never funded a single title. They funded a company with a thesis (auteur cinema, digital-first marketing). The IP portfolio diversified risk.

**Relevance to dezibel:** A24 is aspirational comp for where dezibel could go (Indivizibel label), but the initial raise is funding one IP, not a slate. Different structure needed for Phase 1.

### Annapurna Pictures — The Patron Model (Founded 2011)

- **Funding:** Megan Ellison's personal capital (her father is Larry Ellison, Oracle). No external investors in early stages.
- **Structure:** Direct equity infusions, no bank loans, no VC syndicates. This gave complete creative freedom and ability to absorb high risk on unproven projects.
- **Output:** Lawless, The Master, Zero Dark Thirty, Her, American Hustle — all by 2014.
- **Key insight:** The patron model works when there's a single source of patient capital with no return timeline. Not replicable with angel investors who need an exit path.

### Quinn (Audio Erotica App) — The VC Seed Model

- **Raise:** $3.2M seed round, October 2022.
- **Investors:** Day One Ventures, Global Asset Capital, Great Oaks, TQ Ventures (Scooter Braun), M13 co-founder Courtney Reum. 7 investors total.
- **Structure:** Standard VC seed into a C-corp (Quinn the company, not a single product).
- **Pivot history:** Multiple iterations since 2019 before settling on creator-driven audio marketplace (Spotify-like model).
- **Valuation:** Not publicly disclosed, but $3.2M seed with 7 institutional investors suggests $10-20M post-money range (UNVERIFIED — estimated from typical seed dilution).
- **Key insight:** Quinn raised as a platform/marketplace, not a single piece of content. Investors bought into the company and the distribution model.

### Dipsea (Audio Intimacy/Erotica App) — Seed to Acquisition

- **Seed:** $5.5M, February 2019. Led by Bedrock Capital and Thrive Capital.
- **Series A:** $8.22M, April 2021.
- **Total raised:** $13-15M over 3 rounds.
- **Outcome:** Acquired by RevenueCat, September 2024. Acquisition price not disclosed.
- **Revenue at time of acquisition:** ~$313K annually (per Latka). This is a cautionary data point — significant VC funding, modest revenue.
- **Key insight:** Dipsea raised as a content subscription app, not as a single creative work. Thrive Capital (also A24 investor) led both Dipsea seed and later A24 rounds — this investor has a thesis on content + intimacy.

### Wattpad — Platform for Serialized Fiction

- **Series A (2011):** Led by Khosla Ventures, Yahoo co-founder Jerry Yang, Union Square Ventures, Golden Venture Partners.
- **Total raised:** $117.8M over 5 rounds.
- **Series D (2018):** $51M at ~$398M valuation, led by Tencent.
- **Final valuation:** $500M+.
- **Key insight:** Wattpad funded a platform, not content. The thesis was "mobile is disrupting publishing." Investors bought distribution infrastructure, not IP.

### Serial Box / Realm — Serialized Fiction Startup

- **Seed (2018):** $1.65M, led by Boat Rocker Media.
- **Second seed (2019):** $4.5M, led by Boat Rocker with Forerunner Ventures, Todd Wagner (2929 Entertainment), Uzabase.
- **Total raised:** $18.7M over 4 rounds.
- **Founding team:** Molly Barton (former global digital director, Penguin) and Julian Yap (former DOJ senior counsel).
- **Positioning:** "HBO for Readers" — serialized fiction as episodes.
- **Key insight:** Even "content-first" companies raised as companies, not as single titles. The pitch was the format and the distribution model. Renamed to Realm in 2021 and pivoted to podcasts.

### Critical Role — The Bootstrapped IP Empire

- **External funding:** $0 from outside investors for equity. The 8 founders (cast members) own all equity.
- **Revenue:** ~$18.7M annually, 91 employees.
- **How they funded growth:** (1) Kickstarter for Legend of Vox Machina animated series — $11.3M from 88,887 backers. (2) Licensing deals with Funko, McFarlane Toys, Penguin Random House, Ripple Junction. (3) Beacon streaming service ($5.99/mo). (4) A $0.68M loan in April 2020 (only external capital).
- **Corporate structure:** Critical Role Productions LLC. Divisions include a production studio, publishing arm (Darrington Press), gaming division, streaming service, record label, and charitable foundation.
- **Key insight:** Critical Role proves the IP-first model works — but only when you can bootstrap with free distribution (YouTube/Twitch) and crowdfunding. They never gave up equity to outside investors.

### Welcome to Night Vale / Night Vale Presents — Fan-Funded Network

- **External investment:** None. Entirely fan-supported (Patreon, merchandise via Topatoco, sponsorships, live shows).
- **Structure:** Night Vale Presents is an independent podcast network founded 2015 by Joseph Fink and Jeffrey Cranor.
- **Business model:** Direct fan support, no VC involvement.
- **Key insight:** Another proof that creative IP can scale without equity investors — but requires an existing audience and low production costs. Not applicable when you need $700K+ before launch.

## 3. The Graduation Path — Single IP to Label/Platform

### The Typical Corporate Evolution

**Phase 1: Single-IP Entity**
- LLC or C-corp holds one project and its IP
- Investors have membership units (LLC) or preferred shares (C-corp) or SAFEs
- Revenue from the single product flows through this entity

**Phase 2: The IP Proves Out → Expansion Decision**
Three common paths:

1. **Roll-up into parent entity:** Create a new parent company. Original IP entity becomes a subsidiary. New investors buy into the parent. Original investors either (a) swap their SPV units for parent equity at a negotiated rate, or (b) retain their SPV position and receive returns only from the original IP.

2. **Pro rata rights in follow-on:** If original investors have pro rata rights (standard in SAFEs and preferred equity), they can maintain their ownership percentage by investing proportionally in the next round. This is the cleanest graduation path — investors who believe in the larger vision participate; those who don't retain their original position.

3. **Buyout of original investors:** The creator raises a larger round for the expanded entity and uses proceeds (or new investor capital) to buy out early investors at a negotiated multiple. Common in film — a project SPV's investors get bought out when a studio acquires the franchise.

### Real Examples of Graduation

**Gimlet Media:** Started with $1.5M seed at $10M valuation for a podcast company. Grew to $28.5M total raised at ~$70M valuation. Acquired by Spotify for $230M. Early seed investors got a 23x return on a company that started as essentially one podcast (StartUp, which literally documented its own fundraising).

**Wondery:** Last pre-acquisition valuation was $100M (June 2019). Acquired by Amazon for $300M in 2020. 3x in 18 months for late investors.

**Marvel Entertainment:** The extreme graduation case. Started as a comic book publisher. 1991 IPO at ~$100M (40% of stock sold for $40M). Ronald Perelman described it as "a mini-Disney in terms of intellectual property." Organized into four units: Marvel Studios, Toy Biz, Licensing, Publishing. Eventually acquired by Disney for $4B. Key structural move: Marvel separated character licensing (Marvel Characters Group) from production and publishing — each revenue stream had its own division.

**Lionsgate:** Founded 1997 with $16M from Frank Giustra + $40M from investors. Went public by merging with a Toronto Stock Exchange shell company (Beringer Gold Corp). Used the $64M war chest to acquire Canadian film businesses. Additional $33.1M from Paul Allen, Jon Feltheimer, and others in 2000. Built through acquisition of individual IP and distribution assets.

### What Original Investors Typically Get

- **If SAFE:** Converts at the cap when a priced round happens. If the "graduation" raise is the priced round, SAFE holders convert into preferred equity in the larger entity at their favorable cap.
- **If SPV/LLC:** Operating agreement defines what happens. Smart investors negotiate either (a) pro rata rights in any successor entity, or (b) a liquidation preference that ensures they get paid first from the original IP's revenue regardless of corporate restructuring.
- **If the creator wants to buy out early investors:** Typical is 2-5x return for angel investors in a successful creative venture. Higher multiples require proven revenue.

## 4. SAFE Structure for Dezibel Specifically

### The Math: Post-Money SAFE at $8M Cap, $875K Raise (7 x $125K)

With a post-money SAFE:
- **Each investor's ownership:** $125,000 / $8,000,000 = **1.5625%**
- **Total investor ownership:** $875,000 / $8,000,000 = **10.9375%**
- **Founder ownership post-SAFE:** **89.06%**
- **Post-money SAFEs don't dilute each other** — all 7 SAFEs at the same cap stack cleanly.

### Is $8M Reasonable?

**Arguments for $8M being reasonable:**

1. **The product ecosystem justifies it.** This isn't a single content product. The revenue streams are: $49 core subscription (97% margin), $149 haptic bundle, oBitchuary Substack ($3/mo), Shit Eyes album, Hot Ghost incense, Pyrrha jewelry (revenue share), t-shirts, and a sequel (That Night) already designed. Multiple monetization paths reduce single-point-of-failure risk.

2. **Comparable seed valuations are higher.** Median pre-money seed valuation in 2025-2026 is ~$16M. Angel rounds specifically tend toward 10-15% dilution, which at $875K implies a $5.8M-$8.75M post-money. $8M is right in this range.

3. **The tech component.** Real-time SMS delivery at scale, haptic integration, weather API injection — this is product infrastructure, not just content. Tech-enabled content companies command higher multiples than pure content.

4. **Attached talent.** Jodi Balfour (Ted Lasso), connections to A24/Neon, Oddfellows Pictures ($200M+ global sales) for sequel. These reduce execution risk.

5. **Format precedent doesn't exist.** First-mover in a format (serialized novel via iMessage) has asymmetric upside. If the format works, the label (Indivizibel) is worth multiples of the first title.

**Arguments against / risk factors:**

1. **Pre-revenue.** No paying customers yet. The $8M is entirely forward-looking.
2. **First-time founder (as a venture).** Graeme has artistic track record but no prior startup exit.
3. **Content risk.** The story is ~70% complete. Investors are funding completion risk.
4. **Market validation is theoretical.** The 500 Day 1 subscribers target depends on a Guardian piece. Without it, Day 1 drops to ~145.
5. **Erotic content complicates growth.** Payment processing, app store restrictions, advertising limitations all constrain scale.

**Net assessment:** $8M post-money is defensible but on the aggressive end for a pre-revenue creative product. It's reasonable if pitched as an IP/ecosystem company with tech infrastructure, not as a single content product. The $49 price point at 97% margins is the strongest argument — if 10,000 subscribers materialize, that's $490K revenue from the core product alone in Year 1, before any ecosystem revenue.

### SAFE vs. LLC Operating Agreement for This Raise

**SAFE advantages:**
- Simple, standard, well-understood by angel investors
- No valuation negotiation (cap is the ceiling, not the price)
- Converts cleanly into a priced round later (the "graduation" moment)
- YC standard docs are free and battle-tested

**SAFE complications for this specific raise:**
- SAFEs were designed for C-corps. If dezibel is structured as an LLC, SAFEs must be adapted — there's no "stock" to convert into, so you define equivalent membership interest rights. Legally possible but introduces complexity.
- SAFEs have no maturity date — investors get no return until a conversion trigger (priced round, acquisition, or dissolution). For angel investors expecting revenue from a product launching in 6-12 months, this may feel wrong. They might prefer revenue participation.
- A conversion trigger of "equity financing of at least $X" needs to be set. For a creative company that may never raise a traditional Series A, what triggers conversion? Consider adding a revenue milestone trigger or a time-based conversion (e.g., converts to equity at cap after 24 months regardless).

**Alternative: Revenue-share note with equity kicker**
- Investors get X% of revenue until they've received Y multiple of their investment (e.g., 2x)
- After recoupment, they retain a small equity position (e.g., 2-3%)
- This gives investors cash returns from the product while preserving founder equity for the larger vision (Indivizibel label)
- More complex to draft but better aligned with a single-IP creative product that generates revenue

**Recommendation (UNVERIFIED — requires lawyer):** For 7 angel investors at $125K each into a pre-launch creative IP with multiple revenue streams:
1. **If you plan to raise a priced round within 18-24 months:** Post-money SAFE at $8M cap. Clean, standard, converts into the Series A for the label.
2. **If you plan to self-sustain from revenue:** LLC with revenue-share waterfall and small equity retention. Better aligned with a business that may never raise institutional capital.
3. **Hybrid:** SAFE with a custom provision that adds a revenue-share component until conversion. Non-standard but possible.

### What This Means for Dezibel at $8M

Gimlet at $10M post-money seed is the most relevant comp. Gimlet was one podcast with a company thesis. Dezibel is one novel with a more developed product ecosystem (multiple revenue streams vs. Gimlet's ad-supported single format).

Dipsea at $5.5M seed (est. $20-30M post-money) is also relevant — audio content + intimacy + subscription, similar content category constraints. But Dipsea was a platform from day one, not a single IP.

Quinn at $3.2M seed is relevant for content category (erotica/intimacy) but is a marketplace model, not a single creative work.

**The honest assessment:** There is no perfect comp for dezibel because no one has done this before — a literary novel delivered by iMessage with a haptic vibrator edition, incense line, music album, Substack persona, jewelry partnership, and sequel already designed. The absence of a comp is both the risk (unproven category) and the opportunity (first-mover in a format).

At $8M post-money for $875K, investors are getting 10.9% of:
- A pre-revenue creative IP with 7+ revenue streams designed
- A technology platform (real-time SMS delivery at scale)
- An attached sequel with a potential production partner (Oddfellows, $200M+ in global sales)
- A label thesis (Indivizibel) that could publish other works in the same format
- Format licensing potential if the delivery mechanism proves out

That's a lot of optionality for 10.9%. The question isn't whether $8M is "right" — it's whether the investors believe the format works. If yes, $8M is cheap. If no, any valuation is too high.

## 6. Structural Recommendation for the Raise

### For 7 Angels at $125K = $875K Total

**Recommended structure (subject to lawyer review):**

1. **Entity:** Delaware C-corp or LLC (depending on tax preference — C-corp is cleaner for SAFE conversion; LLC allows revenue pass-through which angels often prefer).

2. **Instrument:** Post-money SAFE at $8M cap with:
   - Standard YC post-money SAFE terms
   - Pro rata rights for all investors (standard — lets them participate in future rounds)
   - Information rights (quarterly updates on subscriber count, revenue, key milestones)
   - Custom addition: if no priced round within 36 months, SAFE converts automatically at the cap

3. **IP ownership:** All dezibel IP (story, characters, brand, technology, ecosystem products) owned by the entity. This is what investors are buying into.

4. **Creator protections:**
   - Managing member / CEO role with creative control explicitly defined
   - Investors have no approval rights over creative decisions
   - Anti-dilution protection for founder in case of future down round

5. **The graduation provision:** If/when Indivizibel (the label) is formed as a separate entity, SAFE holders convert into shares of the label entity, not just the dezibel IP entity. This gives investors upside in the larger vision. Alternatively, define a right-of-first-refusal for SAFE holders to invest in the label entity.

### What Investors Are Actually Buying

Frame this clearly in the SAFE side letter or investor memo:

- **Not:** A share of one novel's revenue
- **Yes:** Equity in a company that owns: (1) a novel delivered via proprietary technology, (2) a subscription product (oBitchuary), (3) a music album, (4) physical product partnerships, (5) a hardware integration, (6) a sequel IP, and (7) the technology and format that could become a label

This framing is the difference between a $3M valuation (single title) and an $8M valuation (IP ecosystem + tech + label thesis).
