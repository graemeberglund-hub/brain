The novel is the acquisition. The engine is the business. Everything I'm rebuilding starts there.

Not by pretending I already know how — by getting the right people around this and building on real data instead of projections I can't defend.

**The engine.**

The sequel ("That Night") is a crime investigation delivered over text. The reader is talking to a character — an AI model trained on my writing, my voice, my characters. They're trying to solve something. When they crack a clue or figure out a riddle, it unlocks a media bundle — pre-shot video, recorded audio, photos, documents. All human-produced. All sitting in a branching story tree that was designed and built before the reader ever starts. The whole thing takes weeks to move through.

The AI is the interface, not the author. Everything the reader unlocks was written, filmed, and produced by people. The AI's job is to inhabit a character well enough that the reader forgets they're inside a structure. From their side, there's no technology — there's a person texting them back.

The six-week narrative is what makes this economically viable. It creates an emotional bond that turns the sequel engine from a chatbot into something people will pay for repeatedly. Dezibel already lives in erotic territory, and in the engine that becomes a real differentiator — pre-written intimate scenarios become interactive, the character responds to the reader in real time, staying inside authored guardrails. Dipsea and Quinn are passive audio. This is participatory — a character the reader already knows, in a scene that was written, responding to them specifically.

**Pulse.**

Pulling it from the budget and the ask. It was always a vendor partnership, not a hardware build — my deck didn't make that clear. Going forward it's a future marketing layer on top of the engine, not a separate company on the same check.

**The pilot.**

100 paid readers, real cohort data — I agree that's the slide that makes everything else credible. Did they finish? Would they pay for the sequel? Did they tell anyone? That data either proves the thesis or kills it, and I'd rather know which.

The production reality: this is multimedia, not software. A credible test means cast, filmed content, recorded audio, sound design, editorial. A stripped-down version doesn't test Dezibel, it tests something else. I'm building a milestone-gated structure around that in the rebuilt deck.

The biggest unresolved question is how the first cohort of paying readers arrives. I'm treating that as the primary thing the pilot and the rebuilt deck need to answer.
